How to use: Work through one day at a time. If life interrupts, pick up where you left off. Check what changed, handle urgent issues and resume the next relevant action. Extend the pace when needed; record any deferral privately. Ticks show your own checklist status, not verified financial health.

W1
Audit — Rebuild the Picture
Income, expenses, spending patterns, net position.
Days 1–7
✓
Day 1Open the system
Open or create “My Money Partner 2026”. State that you are beginning the reset and write one masked sentence about your situation. Privately check bills due before your next income; handle anything urgent now.
✓
Day 2Income audit
Use Chapter 3 to list all income sources, net take-home amounts and actual pay dates. Separate reliable income from variable or uncertain receipts. Convert frequencies consistently and keep a lower-income case.
✓
Day 3Fixed costs audit
List monthly, quarterly and annual commitments, including housing, utilities, insurance, subscriptions and required repayments. Record due dates and monthly provisions; annual bills are not paid monthly just because the budget averages them.
✓
Day 4Variable spending audit
Review at least the last 30 days privately, and a longer period for seasonal costs. Categorise spending; distinguish transfers, refunds and card repayments to avoid counting purchases twice. Share a masked summary, not statements.
✓
Day 5The audit summary
Use Chapter 3’s audit prompts for a dated one-page summary of income, costs and monthly surplus or shortfall, plus what you own and owe. Check totals and missing items privately; a monthly surplus may hide a cash shortage before payday.
✓
Day 6The value audit
Run Chapter 6’s value audit. Identify spending that helps you and spending you could reconsider; use up to three examples of each where relevant. Choose a useful change. Increase a category only if the checked budget can support it.
✓
Day 7Audit reflection
Ask AI which issue the supplied audit suggests addressing first, and why. Check the reasoning against your records and priorities. Record the month’s focus and any support needed; do not postpone urgent action until the next stage.
W2
Implement — Savings and Spending
Check the budget first, then test savings and review spending.
Days 8–14
✓
Day 8Build your budget
Run Chapter 4’s budget-building prompt using the checked audit. Allocate Housing, Essentials, Lifestyle, Goals and Buffer. Include required repayments and irregular-bill provisions once; check totals, cash timing and any shortfall before committing new transfers.
✓
Day 9Budget stress-test
Use Chapter 4’s stress-test with one plausible extra cost, such as 300 in your stated currency, and a lower-income case. Check the arithmetic and available cash. Revise the plan only where justified; protect essentials and required payments.
✓
Day 10Emergency fund calculation
Use Chapter 5’s reserve prompt. Calculate a one-month starter milestone and a longer target suited to your circumstances, or a smaller first goal. Include necessary costs and required payments; keep known-bill money and Buffer separate. [5]
✓
Day 11Set your savings number
Run Chapter 5’s savings starting-point prompt with the checked budget. Choose a genuinely available amount from Goals after other allocations. Check upcoming bills and actual pay timing privately. If nothing is available, record zero and the next useful step.
✓
Day 12Set up the savings transfer
If affordable, set the transfer yourself after income is available. Check destination, timing, fees and existing instructions to avoid duplicates. Save the setup, then check the first completed transfer when due. Variable income may suit a manual transfer. [4]
✓
Day 13Subscription audit
Use Chapter 6’s subscription audit. Review 60 days of transactions and check annual renewals separately. Cancel only an unwanted service after checking terms, notice and fees. Record confirmation; stopping payment alone may not end the contract. [4]
✓
Day 14Week 2 review
Use Chapter 9’s monthly-review framework for the period so far, labelled as a partial month. Confirm actual activity, unresolved checks and the next transfer’s affordability. Address urgent issues; note one routine follow-up rather than forcing a change.
W3
Implement — Debt and Investing
Debt plan automated. Investment contribution running.
Days 15–21
✓
Day 15Complete debt picture
Use Chapter 7 Prompt 1 with dated balances, rates, fees, required payments, due dates and relevant terms. Check totals and supported payoff estimates independently. If debt-free, record that and check for unpaid bills or other obligations. [3]
✓
Day 16Choose your debt strategy
Compare Chapter 7’s avalanche and snowball approaches with the same affordable repayment budget. Protect priority needs and required payments before extra repayment. Verify costs and assumptions; seek support promptly if repayments cannot be met. [2, 3]
✓
Day 17Automate debt repayment
If affordable, set the checked extra payment yourself. Confirm due dates, processing, fees and lender allocation; check existing instructions. Required payments must still be covered. Verify the first actual payment and save a dated estimate, not a guaranteed payoff date.
✓
Day 18Investment starting-point review
Use Chapter 8’s starting-point prompt for education. Check essentials, required payments, accessible reserve, high-interest debt, goal, timeframe and unallocated surplus. Research risks, fees, access and provider legitimacy. AI cannot certify readiness or suitability. [6]
✓
Day 19Set up or defer investing
Proceed only after the checks support your decision. Confirm product or employer-scheme terms and an affordable contribution. Check whether money is actually invested or remains cash. If deferred, record the reason, next step and review date, not a promised readiness date.
✓
Day 20Run the red-team prompt
Run Chapter 10 Prompt 1 with a fresh masked summary. Check relevant concerns independently; the critique is not a financial audit. Act on urgent issues now and set follow-up dates for others. Fewer than three concerns may be justified.
✓
Day 21Week 3 review
Compare all planned transfers with upcoming income, essentials and required payments. Identify a plausible disruption and an affordable contingency, such as reducing an optional transfer. Do not rank likelihood without evidence or count the same reserve twice.
W4
Maintain and Transform
Establish the rituals. Review actual activity and unresolved follow-ups.
Days 22–30
✓
Day 22Set up Money Monday
Choose a weekly day and time that fits your life. Set a recurring reminder and run Chapter 9 Prompt 1 with current records. Cover the period since the last actual check; do not limit the scan to seven days after a longer gap.
✓
Day 23The assumption audit
Run Chapter 10 Prompt 3. Check income, costs, debt, savings and investment assumptions against records. Label estimates and unknowns; record relevant actions. Schedule the monthly review and assumption check, plus the fuller quarterly red-team review.
✓
Day 24Life change update
List actual changes in work, income, housing, health, family or goals since the audit. Use Chapters 3, 4 and 9 to update affected amounts and dates. Check revised instructions and avoid duplicates. If nothing changed, record that rather than inventing an adjustment.
✓
Day 25Savings projection
Use Chapter 5’s twelve-month projection with the current balance, actual contribution dates and expected withdrawals. Check the reserve timeline independently, using zero interest first. Increase a transfer only after a fresh budget and cash-flow check.
✓
Day 26Debt payoff projection
Use Chapter 7’s projection and independent calculation checks. Include fees, rates, payment timing and new charges. Record a dated estimate or why no date is supported. Schedule a progress review in three months; celebrate actual repayment when confirmed.
✓
Day 27Investment projection
If relevant, use Chapter 8’s ten-year scenarios: 6%, 7%, 8%, zero and negative 3%, with explicit contributions, timing and costs. These are hypothetical models, not forecasts or loss limits. Check calculations independently; if deferred, record a research step instead.
✓
Day 28The complete system review
Ask AI to organise the records you have verified. Mark each part completed, pending, deferred or not applicable, with reasons and next dates. A scheduled transfer is not a cleared payment, and a larger investment balance is not automatically a return. [8]
✓
Day 29Schedule the annual review
Choose an annual date using Chapter 9. If using the reset anniversary, put the review about twelve months after Day 1 and a preparation reminder one month beforehand. List records needed. Tax or renewal deadlines may require separate earlier reminders.
✓
Day 30The reset reflection
Ask AI which useful habit the supplied evidence suggests protecting. Make your own choice. Compare the starting note and audit with today’s records; write what helped, what remains difficult and your next step. Bring this reflection into Chapter 12.
